# Digital Product Opportunity Comparison

This comparison synthesizes only the evidence and caveats in `research/opportunity-brief.md`; it adds no new research and does not rank the opportunities. **FACT** means source-supported in that brief, **OBSERVATION** means directly observed there, and **HYPOTHESIS** means unvalidated.

## Five opportunities

### 1. Maintenance Request-to-Resolution Kit
- **Target customer:** Independent property managers (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Problem:** Inconsistent maintenance intake, vendor coordination, and resident follow-up (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Existing demand evidence:** **FACT:** TenantCloud markets paid plans with maintenance-related and operational features, and a maintenance contractor-discovery route [S1–S2]. This supports interest in the workflow, not demand for a separate kit.
- **Competition:** **FACT:** TenantCloud and other property-management platforms address rental operations [S1–S2]. Exact template substitutes were not verified.
- **Pricing signals:** **FACT:** TenantCloud pricing was reviewed, but a numeric comparable price was unavailable in the captured page. Proposed $29–79 is a **HYPOTHESIS**, not a benchmark.
- **Format:** Editable SOP, request form, triage matrix, vendor log, resident messages, closeout checklist.
- **Fulfillment effort:** Potentially low with automatically delivered editable files and clear instructions (OBSERVATION); support burden is unknown.
- **Ongoing maintenance:** Periodic compatibility/link and regulatory review; updates and customization add effort (OBSERVATION).
- **Distribution options:** Search content, property-management communities/newsletters, consultant partnerships, direct outreach (HYPOTHESES).
- **Key assumption to validate:** Managers experience this recurring gap and would pay for/use a separate tracker alongside their existing platform.

### 2. Small-Trade Job Closeout & Callback Kit
- **Target customer:** Small trade contractors, owner-operators, and office managers (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Problem:** Missing job photos or sign-offs and inconsistent callback/root-cause documentation (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Existing demand evidence:** **FACT:** Jobber sells field-service plans and lists workflow automation, job costing, and scheduling features [S3–S4]. This establishes paid software and workflow relevance, not kit demand.
- **Competition:** **FACT:** Jobber and Housecall Pro provide field-service software [S3, S6]. Exact substitutes for the proposed forms/SOP were not verified.
- **Pricing signals:** **FACT:** Jobber lists plans at $29–$529/month in its annual-billing presentation, with configuration affecting price [S3]. These are software prices, not template comparables. Proposed $29–99 is a **HYPOTHESIS**.
- **Format:** Mobile-friendly or printable forms, closeout checklist, callback log, and role-based SOP.
- **Fulfillment effort:** Potentially low-touch digital file delivery (OBSERVATION); usability/support needs are untested.
- **Ongoing maintenance:** Periodic file/link and regulatory review; trade-specific customization could add support (OBSERVATION/HYPOTHESIS).
- **Distribution options:** Search-led checklists, contractor communities/newsletters, consultant partnerships, direct outreach (HYPOTHESES).
- **Key assumption to validate:** The same closeout failure recurs across a sufficiently focused trade segment and the kit fits existing job-management workflows.

### 3. GHL Property-Service Workflow Snapshot
- **Target customer:** GoHighLevel agencies or implementers serving one property-service niche (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Problem:** Repeated setup of pipelines, fields, messages, and workflows (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Existing demand evidence:** **FACT:** GoHighLevel markets CRM/automation tools and operates an app marketplace [S7–S8]. These do not show demand for a snapshot product.
- **Competition:** **FACT:** GHL itself supplies CRM/automation capabilities and an app marketplace [S7–S8]. Exact snapshot competitors were not verified.
- **Pricing signals:** **FACT:** GHL’s pricing page notes usage-based charges may apply [S7]; no comparable snapshot price was verified. Proposed $49–199 is a **HYPOTHESIS**.
- **Format:** Importable workflow snapshot, field map, message templates, setup guide, and QA tests.
- **Fulfillment effort:** Digital delivery is possible, but setup/import questions may create support needs (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Ongoing maintenance:** Highest relative burden in the source brief: account settings, platform behavior, and client processes vary (OBSERVATION).
- **Distribution options:** Agency partnerships, niche implementation content, direct outreach; the marketplace is confirmed, but snapshot sales there are not (HYPOTHESES).
- **Key assumption to validate:** Agencies repeat the same setup enough to pay for a compatible snapshot, and compatibility can be maintained economically.

### 4. Human-Supervised AI Support SOP Pack
- **Target customer:** Small customer-support/operations teams introducing AI (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Problem:** Inconsistent AI responses, quality review, and escalation handling (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Existing demand evidence:** **FACT:** Intercom sells AI support features with per-seat and per-outcome pricing [S5]. This is paid software-category evidence, not evidence for a standalone SOP pack.
- **Competition:** **FACT:** Intercom and other support platforms offer AI-agent features [S5]. Exact SOP-pack substitutes were not verified.
- **Pricing signals:** **FACT:** Intercom lists Essential at $29/seat/month and Fin AI at $0.99 per outcome, with higher tiers available [S5]. These are software prices; proposed $39–129 is a **HYPOTHESIS**.
- **Format:** Knowledge-base template, response rubric, escalation rules, prompt examples, and review log.
- **Fulfillment effort:** Potentially low-touch editable file delivery (OBSERVATION); application-specific guidance may increase support (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Ongoing maintenance:** AI behavior, privacy practices, and workflows may change; review/update needs are unmeasured (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Distribution options:** Search content, support/operations communities, consultant partnerships, direct outreach (HYPOTHESES).
- **Key assumption to validate:** Teams need a vendor-neutral practical governance pack beyond their platform’s documentation and will pay for it.

### 5. Local Delivery Exception Desk Kit
- **Target customer:** Small fleets or local delivery operators (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Problem:** Inconsistent handling of delays, failed deliveries, proof of delivery, and dispatcher handoffs (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Existing demand evidence:** **FACT:** Onfleet is an established delivery-operations product [S9]. The reviewed source did not provide evidence of standalone kit demand.
- **Competition:** **FACT:** Onfleet offers delivery-operations software [S9]. Exact template competitors and substitutes were not verified.
- **Pricing signals:** Numeric Onfleet pricing was unavailable in the captured page; comparable digital-download prices were not verified. Proposed $29–99 is a **HYPOTHESIS**.
- **Format:** Exception decision tree, customer scripts, proof checklist, incident log, and dispatcher handoff SOP.
- **Fulfillment effort:** Potentially low-touch editable file delivery (OBSERVATION); fit with dispatch tools needs testing.
- **Ongoing maintenance:** Periodic compatibility and regulatory review; operational variation may require customization (OBSERVATION/HYPOTHESIS).
- **Distribution options:** Search content, local-operator communities, consultant partnerships, direct outreach (HYPOTHESES).
- **Key assumption to validate:** Operators have an uncovered recurring exception-handling problem not already solved by their dispatch software.

## Evidence assessment

### Well-supported by the collected evidence
- **FACT:** Named software providers offer products in property management, field service, CRM/automation, AI customer support, and delivery operations [S1–S9].
- **FACT:** Jobber and Intercom publish specific recurring software prices; the brief records Jobber at $29–$529/month (annual-billing presentation) and Intercom Essential at $29/seat/month plus Fin AI at $0.99 per outcome [S3, S5].
- **OBSERVATION:** These product pages show commercially packaged workflow capabilities. They establish adjacent category activity, not sales volume, customer outcomes, or willingness to buy independent templates.
- **OBSERVATION:** Automatically delivered editable files could reduce fulfillment effort; compatibility, support, customization, and update work can undermine that potential.

### Still hypotheses
- The proposed customer segments have the described recurring pain and will pay for these specific products.
- The listed workflow gaps exist beyond current platform features and free templates.
- Suggested distribution channels can reach buyers repeatedly at sustainable effort or cost.
- Any proposed product price range, repeat-purchase potential, differentiation, or low-maintenance economics.
- The concepts can be created once and used across customers without substantial customization.

### Important missing information
- Interviews or observed workflow evidence from each intended customer segment; frequency and cost of the problem.
- Comparable digital-download competitors, actual prices, sales evidence, and buyer reviews.
- Willingness to pay, conversion/acquisition costs, support volume, refund likelihood, and sales/maintenance economics.
- Channel reach and eligibility; platform compatibility tests for a GHL snapshot.
- Specific regulatory, privacy, safety, and jurisdictional requirements for each chosen workflow.

### Validate before building
1. Select a customer segment and workflow to investigate—not a product winner—and interview 5–8 people in that segment about recent real incidents, current workarounds, and spending.
2. Verify direct downloadable alternatives, their prices, and available customer feedback; distinguish observations from claims about sales.
3. Show a small sample artifact and test commitment with a paid preorder or refundable pilot before producing the full kit.
4. Test fit with actual tools and users. For GHL, import into multiple clean subaccounts; for AI support, test human-reviewed scenarios and escalation paths.
5. Estimate support, updates, fulfillment, and channel effort from the pilot. Do not infer profitable or “passive” sales from stated interest alone.
