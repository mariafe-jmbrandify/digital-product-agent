# Validation Plan

Based on `opportunity-brief.md` and `opportunity-comparison.md`, these three opportunities have comparatively concrete evidence of paid software categories and/or published pricing. They are **not ranked**. Such evidence does not prove demand for standalone digital products, profitability, or low-maintenance sales.

## Small-Trade Job Closeout & Callback Kit

1. **Evidence — FACT:** Jobber sells field-service software with workflow and job-management features; its current pricing page shows paid monthly plans that vary by team size and billing term [1, 2]. Housecall Pro is another field-service platform [3]. This is software spend, not template demand.
2. **Unproven assumption — HYPOTHESIS:** A specific trade has recurring closeout/callback gaps not solved by its software or free forms, and will pay for a reusable kit.
3. **Validating/weakening evidence:** Operators independently describe recent documentation or handoff failures and show their workaround; a concrete paid-pilot commitment validates more than stated interest. Rare problems, adequate software, or demand only for customization weaken the case.
4. **Where to look:** Interview owner-operators/office managers in one trade; inspect redacted closeout records and current workflows. Review Jobber, Housecall Pro, other tools they use, help content, user discussions, and actual spending on forms/training/implementation.
5. **Competitors to examine:** Jobber, Housecall Pro, trade-specific tools used by interviewees, and downloadable closeout/checklist templates. Check included features, extra costs, adoption, and reviews—not just listing claims.
6. **What customers appear to pay for — FACT:** Jobber publishes recurring prices for field-service software [1]. No comparable standalone SOP/form price or sales evidence was verified.
7. **Smallest test format:** Private rough one-page closeout checklist plus one callback-log example.
8. **Low-cost experiment:** Interview 8 operators in one trade about their last 3 jobs/callbacks. Show the rough page and test the brief’s $29–99 range as a hypothetical one-time price; ask for a specific written commitment to a paid pilot if it solves the observed problem.
9. **Proceed to creation if:** Suggested screening rule—not market evidence—at least 5/8 report the same recent recurring gap and 3 unrelated operators commit to a paid pilot in-range; the core workflow must work without customization.
10. **Abandon/modify if:** Fewer than 2/8 report the same gap, software already handles it, nobody commits after 8 qualified interviews, or trade-specific customization is essential. Narrow the trade or workflow and retest.

## Human-Supervised AI Support SOP Pack

1. **Evidence — FACT:** Intercom sells AI-enabled support with published per-seat and per-outcome pricing; its current page lists Essential at $29/seat/month and Fin AI from $0.99 per outcome [4]. This shows paid support/AI software, not demand for SOP packs.
2. **Unproven assumption — HYPOTHESIS:** Small teams need reusable quality-review and escalation guidance beyond their platform’s documentation, and will pay for vendor-neutral material.
3. **Validating/weakening evidence:** Look for recent AI answer/handoff incidents, recurring manual QA, and current time/tool/consulting spend. Paid-pilot intent validates; teams not using AI, relying on built-in controls, or requiring bespoke compliance work weakens the case.
4. **Where to look:** Interview support leads already using AI; request anonymized QA rubrics, escalation policies, and examples of recent incidents. Review Intercom and the platforms they actually use, including documentation, add-ons, procurement, and implementation spend.
5. **Competitors to examine:** Intercom Fin, respondents’ support-platform AI/QA features, vendor-provided templates, and support-operations consultants or training.
6. **What customers appear to pay for — FACT:** Intercom publishes seat and AI-outcome pricing [4]. No standalone SOP-pack price or sales evidence was verified.
7. **Smallest test format:** Private one-page escalation rubric and QA scorecard applied to one anonymized scenario.
8. **Low-cost experiment:** Interview 8 AI-using support leads; walk through one recent failure and compare their review process with the rough rubric. Test the brief’s $39–129 hypothesis range through a written paid-pilot commitment; do not use customer data or deploy prompts.
9. **Proceed to creation if:** Suggested rule: 5/8 confirm a repeated gap not covered by their platform, 3 unrelated teams commit to a paid pilot, and one core rubric fits without sensitive data or bespoke compliance advice.
10. **Abandon/modify if:** Most have not deployed AI, existing controls suffice, no one commits, or each buyer needs platform/security/legal customization. Narrow to one platform or workflow if interviews support it.

## Maintenance Request-to-Resolution Kit

1. **Evidence — FACT:** TenantCloud describes paid tiers with maintenance and property-operation capabilities and provides a maintenance contractor-discovery route [5, 6]. Numeric pricing was unavailable in the captured page. This is category/workflow evidence, not tracker demand.
2. **Unproven assumption — HYPOTHESIS:** Independent managers have recurring intake/vendor/resident handoff gaps outside their property software and will pay for a separate editable kit.
3. **Validating/weakening evidence:** Managers show recent repeated delays, lost requests, vendor coordination failures, or manual workarounds and make a paid-pilot commitment. Built-in software coverage, infrequent incidents, or high customization needs weaken it.
4. **Where to look:** Interview independent managers about recent tickets; inspect redacted request logs and approval/status steps. Examine TenantCloud and interviewees’ actual platforms, included maintenance features, help materials, and existing admin/implementation spend.
5. **Competitors to examine:** TenantCloud and managers’ current property platforms; compare request intake, vendor assignment, updates, approvals, and reporting. Check marketplace trackers/SOPs for direct downloadable substitutes.
6. **What customers appear to pay for — FACT:** TenantCloud markets paid Starter, Growth, Pro, and Business tiers with maintenance/operations features [5]. Numeric prices and comparable template purchases were not verified.
7. **Smallest test format:** Private rough request-to-resolution flow: one sample form, tracker row, and handoff checklist.
8. **Low-cost experiment:** Interview 8 independent managers who oversee maintenance. Reconstruct their last 3 tickets, show the rough flow, and test the brief’s $29–79 range using a written paid-pilot commitment.
9. **Proceed to creation if:** Suggested rule: 5/8 identify the same recurring gap outside their software, 3 unrelated managers commit to a paid pilot, and the workflow needs no custom integrations or jurisdiction-specific advice.
10. **Abandon/modify if:** Software covers the issue, it is infrequent, nobody commits after 8 qualified interviews, or buyers require custom vendor networks/integrations/legal advice. Narrow the workflow or target a platform if warranted.

## Decision guardrails

The interview and commitment thresholds are proposed screening rules, not facts or guarantees. Treat interest, compliments, waitlists, and category-level software spending as weak signals. Before building, verify direct substitutes and downloadable prices, confirm repeated real-world pain/current spend, and obtain credible paid intent. Require human approval before product creation.
