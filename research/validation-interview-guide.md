# Maintenance Workflow Validation Interview Guide

*For exploratory conversations with property managers. Ask about recent real examples, not idealized or hypothetical behavior. Do not imply that the prototype is proven or ask for confidential tenant information.*

## Problem discovery

1. **Current process:** Think about the most recent maintenance request you handled. How did it arrive, and what happened from intake through resolution?
2. **Delays:** In that request or another recent one, where did it wait the longest? What was it waiting on, and how did you know?
3. **Lost information:** Can you recall a recent time request details, access instructions, photos, approval, or status information was missing or hard to find? What happened next?
4. **Reopened work:** When was the last time a work order had to be reopened or a technician had to return? What caused that, and how was it recorded?
5. **Vendor / technician communication:** How do you currently share scope, access details, updates, and completion notes with vendors or technicians? Where does communication break down, if anywhere?
6. **Estimates and approvals:** Walk me through a recent repair that required an estimate or approval. How was the estimate received, who approved it, and what caused any wait?
7. **Documentation:** What records do you expect before closing a work order? Tell me about a recent case where documentation was incomplete or difficult to retrieve.
8. **Software fit:** Which property-management software or tools do you use for maintenance? What parts of this workflow do they handle well, and what still happens outside the system?

## Workflow reaction

9. After the problem discussion, show the rough prototype only if useful: **What, if anything, would not fit your actual process?** Which fields or steps would you remove, change, or add? Ask for concrete examples rather than general approval.

## Willingness-to-pay follow-up

Ask these only after the participant has described a real problem and current workaround:

- What do you currently spend—in staff time, vendor coordination, software, training, or outside help—to manage this workflow?
- Have you paid for a template, setup service, training, or another solution for this problem? What did you pay for, and what happened?
- If a reusable workflow/template addressed the specific gap you described, what would need to be included for you to consider paying for it?
- What budget or purchasing process would apply? Who would approve a one-time purchase, and what alternatives would that budget compete with?
- Would you be willing to take a concrete next step, such as reviewing a pilot scope or making a conditional paid-pilot commitment? What would you need to see first?

Do not ask “Would you buy this?” or treat compliments, stated interest, or an unprompted price guess as proof. Record actual past spending separately from hypothetical intent. Do not collect payment, publish the prototype, or contact vendors/residents as part of this guide.
